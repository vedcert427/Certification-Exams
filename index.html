Preparation guide

A practical routine that works for most technical and professional certifications.

Define your objectives

Write down why you are taking the exam and download the official objective list. Every study session should map to one objective.

Build a schedule

Pick an exam date, count the weeks, and assign topics to each week. Short daily sessions beat occasional long ones.

Concepts before memorization

Understand why a feature exists and what problem it solves. Facts stick better when they hang on a clear mental model.

Use official documentation

Vendor documentation and learning paths are the most accurate source for terminology, limits and current behavior.

Practice hands on

Use a free tier, trial or local lab. Building and breaking small setups teaches more than rereading notes.

Practice questions, responsibly

Use practice questions to find gaps, not to guess patterns. Always read why an answer is correct and look up the concept.

Find weak areas

Keep a log of missed topics. Group them by objective and give the weakest areas extra time each week.

Run timed sessions

Practice under realistic time limits to learn pacing, and learn to skip, flag and return to difficult items.

Track your progress

Use a simple checklist or spreadsheet with confidence ratings per objective, updated after every session.

Revisit hard topics

Return to difficult material after a few days. Spaced review moves knowledge into long term memory.

Final preparation

In the last days, review summary notes, confirm exam logistics, rest well, and avoid learning brand new topics.
